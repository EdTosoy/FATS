# Moderated private beta usability plan

Status: prepared, **not conducted**. Intended participants: 5–10 fresh graduates or first-time job seekers, including people with little experience organizing applications. Run sequential 60–75 minute sessions on an isolated local installation with supplied synthetic data. Start with five; if material changes follow, report the next five separately rather than combining different versions into one success rate. This small qualitative study does not establish population-level usability or production security.

This plan permits rehearsal with fictional records only. It does not authorize recruitment messages, real document collection, remote hosting, public tunnels, AI use or recording participants without separate consent. See [readiness and unresolved blockers](PRIVATE_BETA_READINESS.md).

## Preparation and safety gates

The moderator and maintainer complete one full dry run before invitations:

1. Use a fresh, isolated FATS workspace and browser profile per participant. Do not use the maintainer's account, development records, normal downloads folder or current browser workspace. Keep the server/database/inbox loopback-only. Configure the exact origin, migrated PostgreSQL schema, local mail and persistent private `FATS_DOCUMENTS_DIR` following [local configuration](LOCAL_CONFIGURATION.md) and [documents](DOCUMENTS.md). A test run's temporary directory is not the study's durable storage setup.
2. Keep `FATS_AI_MATCHING_ENABLED=0`, provider/key settings unset, and restart the server. In a synthetic account check that application details contain no Resume comparison panel and GET/POST `/api/matching` return `403 MATCHING_DISABLED`. Never run matching or evaluation commands. An accidental AI request is an immediate stop condition.
3. Prepare only generated fixture files using `tests/helpers/document-fixtures.ts`: two distinguishable PDF resume versions, one DOCX cover letter, and two PDF offer letters. Include “SYNTHETIC — USABILITY TEST” in visible text and filenames. No contact details, signatures, IDs, real employer terms or embedded active content. Provide a plainly invalid `.txt` file to demonstrate rejection; do not use malware or weaponized files.
4. Prepare two permitted synthetic job pages from `tests/fixtures/jobs` for Sidekick: one structured listing and one partial listing needing correction. Serve these locally in the rehearsal or use moderator-controlled routing as in extension tests. Never capture a participant's private account page. Load the verified unpacked Sidekick package, but record installation/pinning assistance separately from task execution. Participants must still initiate capture themselves. Only the two local FATS origins on port 3000 are supported.
5. Give each participant an alias (`P01`…`P10`), unique `@example.test` mailbox and unique study-only passphrase. Registration must begin with a new account. Moderator privately retrieves only that alias's code from local Mailpit and gives it to the participant; never expose the inbox UI, other messages or credentials. Do not store passphrases/codes in observation notes. Study scheduling/contact details stay outside FATS and separate from the alias-based results.
6. Prepare a second empty synthetic restore account. Explicitly label it **Restore practice**; make the participant sign out of the first before using it. Keep the source account intact. Use a different profile for the next participant. Do not reset data by importing an empty legacy backup over protected offer history.
7. Record the code revision plus uncommitted readiness changes, browser/OS, viewport/zoom, fixture version, server configuration state (no secrets), and run date/timezone. Confirm same-day deadlines use Asia/Manila. The moderator sets all scenario dates relative to the session date; examples below are illustrative, not hard-coded current dates.
8. Rehearse a backup/restore and failed/cancelled action, verify matching stays unavailable, check synthetic downloads, and agree support/stop/cleanup ownership. If the setup lacks private storage or any task is broken, postpone invitations and log the failure. Do not clear authentication rate limits during a session to hide friction; wait for the stated cooldown and record it.

## Consent and privacy script

“We are testing FATS, not testing you. The employer, job, pay and documents in this session are fictional. Please use only the supplied information and files. Do not enter your real resume, address, phone number, salary, employer documents or personal account password. You may skip any task, take a break or stop without explaining why. Saving a job does not apply; recording acceptance does not contact an employer. AI is disabled. We will write task observations under your participant code.”

Explain who can access notes, where they are stored, and how to contact the moderator or withdraw feedback. Obtain explicit opt-in separately for any audio/screen recording; refusal does not prevent participation. Default to written alias-based notes without recording. Keep password, inbox and reset-link entry out of recordings and screenshots. Ask before quoting feedback, and remove identifying details. Do not ask for personal job-search stories to fill gaps in fixtures.

Before recruitment, agree a short retention schedule and owner: proposed maximum **14 days after the final session** for raw alias-linked notes, optional recordings, synthetic accounts, files and exports; keep only de-identified themes/aggregate task measurements afterward. This is a proposed study policy, not an implemented automatic deletion service. Participant consent/contact records require their own restricted handling. Explain withdrawal and actual backup-retention limits honestly; do not promise instant erasure from archives. Real-data erasure remains a readiness blocker.

## Session structure and measurement

Allow 5 minutes for introduction/consent, approximately 45–50 for tasks, a short optional break and 10 minutes for feedback. Show one scenario at a time. Invite think-aloud observations without teaching navigation. After about 60 seconds of a stall ask “What are you looking for?”; when the task cap is reached offer one neutral cue, then stop or assist. Record help rather than treating assisted completion as independent success.

Start a monotonic timer when the scenario is read and stop when the participant says they are done and the moderator verifies the saved result/reload. Log elapsed time, active time, system/inbox waits, help and outcome separately. Caps below are planning targets, not observed performance or pass guarantees. Code outcomes **U** (correct unaided), **A** (correct with assistance), **F** (incorrect/not completed) or **S** (skipped/withdrawn); mark any privacy/data-integrity misunderstanding separately as critical. Count missing/conflicting records as failure even if the participant believes the task succeeded.

Do not rush consent or silently label unattempted tasks successful. Optional short recovery task: sign out, request a reset for the synthetic account, use the moderator-provided message, sign in and verify existing records. Never reset a participant's real account or include a token in notes.

## Accessibility and facilitator observations

Include participants who prefer keyboard navigation or larger text where feasible; ask about access needs without collecting health details. Repeat a short save/cancel/offer-confirmation path with keyboard only. Observe visible focus, label comprehension, error announcements, modal focus containment/return, reachability of disclosure sections and absence of horizontal scrolling at narrow widths/200% zoom. A moderator may use a locally available screen reader with separate consent and record product/version; untested tools remain untested, not implied coverage.

Record exact points of hesitation: initial Settings landing, mobile navigation, required location/link, meaning of Saved, manually choosing submitted v1, leaving unsaved offer forms to upload, identifying current vs historical terms, date-only cutoff uncertainty, whether “acceptance” sounds legally binding, and whether a JSON backup sounds like a document archive. Separate a product problem from environment delay and facilitation error. Do not infer that a PDF canvas is fully accessible because its surrounding controls have names.

## Feedback questions

- What did you expect to happen when you saved a job? What would you do next?
- Where did you hesitate or need to reread something? Which words would you change?
- How confident are you, from 1–5, that the correct resume and offer letter are attached? Show which version you would retrieve.
- What would you do if the response deadline passed, or if FATS said your session ended while editing?
- What does recording acceptance/decline do? How would you tell the employer?
- What would your exported file recover after losing this computer? What would still be missing?
- After each major task: how easy was that, 1 (very difficult) to 7 (very easy)? What made it hard?
- What one change would most help with a first application? Would you want to use this again, and what would you need to trust it?

Do not lead with “Was it simple?” or ask for legal interpretation, salary advice, AI evaluation or real contract review.

Proposed release discussion gates: no privacy/cross-account/data-loss incident, no AI request, no uncorrected belief that Save applies or acceptance contacts an employer; all participants can identify backup file limits after the task; at least four of the first five complete save/review/decision/export-preview without procedural help. These are study targets to discuss after observation, not current results. Any readiness Blocking finding still prevents a real-data beta even if usability targets are met.

## Stop, cleanup and follow-up

Stop immediately for real personal files/text, another account's data, unexpected network/model traffic, lost/conflicting history, unsafe downloads or distress. Avoid screenshots of the exposure, stop interacting with affected data, restrict access, and notify the maintainer privately with minimal metadata. Keep relevant originals for safe investigation; do not improvise deletion or restore into the source workspace.

At the end sign out, close the isolated profile and collect only the consented alias-based observations. Do not retain exported JSON in a shared Downloads folder. After investigation/retention obligations are resolved, the maintainer removes **only the explicitly identified disposable study workspace and generated file/export/inbox set** according to the agreed retention schedule; deletion of accounts with document history is not implemented as a normal product action. Keep normal development/user databases untouched. This document is a plan, not authorization to destroy any current data.

Debrief the maintainer after each session, fix critical bounded defects before continuing, and summarize themes after five participants. Keep remaining infrastructure/security work tied to the readiness IDs rather than starting a new phase. No participant sessions or personal document collection were carried out while preparing this plan.
